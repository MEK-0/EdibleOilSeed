ABSTRACT

This study presents a hybrid classification framework for distinguishing six edible oil seed classes using fixed deep convolutional representations and conventional machine learning. The data set contains 10,111 images of black sesame, groundnut, mustard, soybean, sunflower and white sesame. ImageNet-pretrained VGG16 and InceptionV3 produced 512- and 2048-dimensional feature vectors, which were classified using support vector machine, random forest, k-nearest neighbor, gradient boosting and multilayer perceptron algorithms. Under stratified 10-fold cross-validation, VGG16-MLP achieved 99.8318% accuracy and 99.8340% macro F1-score. A feature-space group-aware stress test was then used to examine whether highly similar observations distributed across folds explained the near-perfect performance. Under group-aware 5-fold validation, VGG16-MLP retained 99.7429% accuracy and InceptionV3-SVM retained 99.6143%. The limited decrease indicates that simple near-duplicate leakage was not the principal explanation for the observed performance. Pseudo-grouping nevertheless does not replace acquisition-session grouping or independent external validation.

Keywords: edible oil seeds, VGG16, InceptionV3, deep feature extraction, machine learning, group-aware validation

1. INTRODUCTION

Agricultural products are commonly classified according to species, variety, size, shape, color and quality. Accurate classification directly affects sorting, packaging, storage and quality control. Manual inspection can be sufficient for small quantities, but it becomes slower and less consistent as production volume increases. These limitations have encouraged the use of machine vision and artificial intelligence for rapid, objective and repeatable agricultural product analysis.

Earlier agricultural image studies generally combined handcrafted color, texture, geometric or morphological descriptors with conventional classifiers. More recently, convolutional neural networks have reduced the need to define image features manually. A pretrained CNN can be fine-tuned end to end or used as a fixed feature extractor whose output is classified by another learning algorithm. The latter strategy creates a practical bridge between deep representation learning and classical machine learning.

VGG16 and InceptionV3 represent different architectural principles. VGG16 uses a regular sequence of 3 × 3 convolutions, whereas InceptionV3 applies parallel operations at multiple spatial scales. When their original classification heads are removed and global average pooling is used, the networks generate compact descriptors suitable for SVM, tree ensembles, neighborhood methods and multilayer perceptrons.

The six classes examined here are black sesame, groundnut, mustard, soybean, sunflower and white sesame. Their visual characteristics range from clearly distinct shape and color patterns to partially similar seed structures. The data are also unbalanced: groundnut contains 3,000 observations while white sesame contains 661. Accuracy is therefore interpreted together with macro precision, macro recall and macro F1-score.

Near-perfect image-level performance requires careful interpretation. Standard cross-validation can be optimistic if exact duplicates, near-duplicate frames or several photographs from one physical sample are divided between training and test folds. The saved arrays did not contain physical-seed or acquisition-session identifiers. The revised experiment therefore adds a feature-space group-aware validation stress test without repeating CNN inference.

2. MATERIAL AND METHOD

The workflow consisted of data verification, preprocessing documentation, fixed deep feature extraction, machine learning classification, standard cross-validation and group-aware robustness analysis. Publication-stage experiments reused the saved feature and label arrays; no CNN was retrained and image feature extraction was not repeated.

2.1. Edible Oil Seed Data Set

The complete feature-label arrays contain 10,111 observations. VGG16 has a shape of 10,111 × 512 and InceptionV3 has a shape of 10,111 × 2048. Both arrays are float32 and contain no NaN or infinite values.

2.2. Image Preprocessing

In the original pipeline, images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels, filtered with a 3 × 3 Gaussian kernel, converted to float32 and divided by 255. These operations were documented but not repeated during the publication-stage classifier experiments.

2.3. Deep Feature Extraction

ImageNet-pretrained VGG16 and InceptionV3 were used without their original classification heads. Global average pooling produced 512 features per VGG16 observation and 2,048 features per InceptionV3 observation.

2.4. Validation Protocol

The primary estimate used StratifiedKFold with 10 splits, shuffle enabled and random_state=42. The same fold indices were reused for every model. Accuracy, macro precision, macro recall and macro F1 were recorded for each fold. Out-of-fold predictions were aggregated so that every observation received one prediction while held outside its training subset.

3. CLASSIFICATION MODELS

Five supervised classifiers were selected to cover complementary decision mechanisms. Linear SVM tests large-margin separability; Random Forest represents bagged tree ensembles; KNN measures local neighborhood structure; Gradient Boosting constructs a sequential ensemble; and MLP learns a nonlinear boundary on fixed features. No hyperparameter search was performed.

The hybrid design separates representation from the final decision rule. Once the deep descriptors are stored, multiple classifiers and validation strategies can be tested without repeating CNN inference. Feature dimension alone is not a guarantee of quality: the 512-dimensional VGG16 representation proved more effective for four of the five classifiers.

5. GROUP-AWARE VALIDATION

True physical-seed and capture-session identifiers were unavailable. Feature vectors were therefore L2-normalized and analyzed separately inside each class using cosine similarity. Connected observations were merged with a Union-Find procedure, ensuring that transitive near-duplicate relationships received one group identifier. Every group was verified to contain a single class label.

The selected similarity threshold was 0.9999 for VGG16 and 0.995 for InceptionV3. The resulting pseudo-groups were supplied to StratifiedGroupKFold with five splits, shuffle enabled and random_state=42.

6. DISCUSSION

The experiments show that fixed deep CNN representations contain highly discriminative information for the six seed classes. VGG16-MLP gave the strongest standard estimate, while linear SVM was most effective with InceptionV3. A larger descriptor was therefore not automatically superior.

If simple near-duplicate leakage were the dominant source of performance, constraining highly similar vectors to one fold would be expected to produce a substantial decrease. Instead, VGG16-MLP retained 99.7429% accuracy despite 1,185 observations belonging to multi-sample groups. Strong class separability and effective pretrained representations are more plausible explanations within the available data.

This conclusion remains conditional. Images from the same camera, background or lighting session can share nuisance patterns without exceeding a strict cosine threshold. Conversely, different images may become close in feature space. Thresholds that are too strict leave related observations as singletons; thresholds that are too permissive may merge broad within-class neighborhoods.

The high macro metrics and class-level error profile show that class imbalance alone does not explain the result. Groundnut contains nearly five times as many observations as white sesame, yet white sesame achieved perfect recall. Remaining errors are concentrated among visually related seed appearances rather than being distributed randomly.

7. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK

The raw images were unavailable in the final publication rerun, so preprocessing and CNN inference were not repeated end to end. Physical-seed and acquisition-session identifiers were also absent. Labels confirm class counts but cannot prove exact row correspondence between independently saved VGG16 and InceptionV3 arrays.

The central remaining limitation is the absence of an independent external test set. A stronger experiment should reserve images collected in a new session using different cameras, illumination, backgrounds, distances and seed batches. These images must not participate in model selection or similarity-threshold selection.

A future acquisition protocol should record stable image paths, checksums, physical sample identifiers and session metadata. Performance should then be reported with per-class metrics, confidence intervals and an error analysis covering visually similar seeds, occlusion, background interference and scale change.

8. CONCLUSION

A hybrid framework combining ImageNet-pretrained VGG16 and InceptionV3 representations with five machine learning classifiers was evaluated for six-class edible oil seed recognition. Under standard stratified 10-fold cross-validation, VGG16-MLP achieved 99.8318% accuracy and 99.8340% macro F1-score, with only 17 out-of-fold errors among 10,111 observations.

Class-wise cosine-similarity grouping and StratifiedGroupKFold produced 99.7429% accuracy for VGG16-MLP and 99.6143% for InceptionV3-SVM. The small decreases indicate that simple near-duplicate leakage was not the primary driver of performance within the saved feature spaces. These values remain internal estimates, not claims of universal real-world accuracy.
